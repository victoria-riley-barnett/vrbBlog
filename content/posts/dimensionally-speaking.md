So what the fuck are they on about? (mech interp piece attack??)

for a simplified motivation, consider what a LLM is doing when it is using bash:

turn order desc

*bash* tool uses are function applications with operative parameters! But model tool calls aren't, whats the difference?

Immanence vs objectification, set vs category theoretics lies here. *how* do we need to make our result knowable to LLM / human? 

Why pass it back and forth like this?

machine learning was already concerned with our most general non-linear and most general linear ways to model things, and they coherent combination into *models*, compositions of these two, along with a plan for how to judge our composition. 

In this way, the levels of abstraction start to get slightly strange: why did we need to stop where we did? And following this line of thought has led to the development of residual capture architectures.

This is most clearly followed with the cost story of Deeepseek's MOE models - it *was* a directly alternative strategy just a year or two ago, and then the architecture again flipped in favor of residual capture. That is, there is a cat and mouse arms race of:

lets represent something to model it

lets represent that something more dynamically

we can now perfectly attend to a qkv of it

but because of *that*, we flattened something, so we have to get that back out:

mech interp

Now, when you *know* how to 'get something back out' in mech interp, this is *relatively* close to knowing how to 'put it back in' in terms of making our representation more dynamic - you should probably see where this is going! This is a loop! It has no exit condition, it only accrues from our previous steps

So, is Machine Learning trivial?

Yes, in one sense. Strictly no in another.
