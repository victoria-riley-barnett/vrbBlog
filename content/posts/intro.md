My name is Victoria - I'm a machine learning lecturer at San Francisco State University.   

I *really* hate markdown *files*, but i love the format and have used it for years. Prior to now (a great reorganization around my latest research focus), I ran my personal blog following a **Jamstack-like** methodology and playinng with {so and sos} AHA stack: astro, htmx, alpine.js. 

Executed togther, the three form a kind of 'catch and release' strategy I really liked - instead of worrying about state, I could tell what was wrong with my blog directly (? clarify sell) 

Most LLM interaction paradigms rely on insertion into an agents context. There is a growing subset of field of computer science who are even trying to rewrite langauge and frameworks for this kind of approach (see: Beam, Jev, etc)

Even at its absolute best, there is a fundamental problem with wanting to tie development state, that is, our evolving understanding of a project or idea, alongside the state of that project *as* a tool. 

Now, notetaking is treated as the solution to the above, and it works about as well as you can be disciplined to follow it. That is, a personal note taking system of any kind should be roughly concieved of as a literalization of some order of your thought, introducing the frailty of category logic: we now are keeping track of *two* things! And they must necessarily diverge. 

Wouldn't it be nice if we had a programming language where we could just think about both:\
\- the things we want to impart effect on\
\- the methods we use to impart effects

Enterr Vau

Now, this problematic is resolved by treating it as a record - of course. That's the solution literarture and language chose. Xanadu had this core realization. CRDTs already exist, and are a it seems to me that the largest innovations in computer science have been one of the record, rather than the type. 

Presented this way, we are to understand representation in machine learning as perhaps an example of how we might do this kind of thinking, without types. A contemporary model has components within it whose *precise* design and mechanism is not know to humans (necessarily, I hope to show)  but whose semantic function is - that is, we can see models accrue and then make use of residuals, and in clean convolutional image networks\*\*\*(a series of heavy asterisks, I also hope to show), have even largely cleared up some major feature interpretablility issues (nick hype and aura mech interp post that needs attacked)

This was always my problem with 'projects' for a software engineer, and something I am clearly departing from here. I think it is much more important to see engineering, analysis, or systems reasoning than a github repo and a screenshot. 

in vscode, the state lives with workspaces, languages, file types, agents, and so on -- there is no room for a kind of emergent interaction between parts that we might be interested in, a kind that simplifies the process of a human needing to use a computer and helps direct them towards whatever their ends are.
