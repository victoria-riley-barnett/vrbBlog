I designed and teach CSC 411, an interdisciplinary machine learning course at San Francisco State University. Notebooks are in the [course repository](https://github.com/victoria-riley-barnett/intermediate-ml), and the architecture day handout is embedded below. I plan to release the course materials under a permissive license sometime following the end of the semester.

The course generalizes critical representation, computing complexity, and model evaluation concepts to non-computer scientists as part of SF State's Promoting Inclusivity in Computing [(PINC)](https://www.sfsu.edu/departments/promoting-inclusivity-computing) initiative, a pathway for life-science majors to earn a minor in Computing Applications.

The official course title is CSC 411: Intermediate Machine Learning for Interdisciplinary Data Scientists. It is the second to last course in the minor sequence, positioned between the most data science intensive course of the sequence (CSC 408: Machine Learning and Data Science for Personalized Medicine) and the most computer science intensive (CSC 511: Protein Modeling with Deep Learning). It aims to bridge the two through this general scheme:

1. A lecture series that follows the historical development of machine perception.
2. Labs that begin with no libraries at all, and build up to higher abstractions using Lightning and PyTorch throughout the semester.
3. Tying (1) and (2) together across a more traditional architectural and key-algorithmic retrospective;
4. Doing (3), to the maximum extent possible, in a domain-specific way that suits the cohort, ideally building bridged engineering thinking for architecting frontier applied ml specifically;
5. Capping the course with a final project that requires explicit sequence analysis.

The general goal of my course rewrite was to help scientists apply the latest in machine learning to their research, allowing them to rapidly generalize key concepts so they can get more out of the final course in the sequence: an extremely rich transformers, PCA, and alphafold course taught by a Genentech Principal.
